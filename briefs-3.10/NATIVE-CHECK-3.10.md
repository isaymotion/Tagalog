# Usap Tayo 3.10.0: choices for Dr. Navarro (native check)
All new Tagalog is a draft. These are the places where reviewers saw two natural choices.

## Body, clothes, daily life (picture sets)
- puwit or pwet as the main word
- palasingsingan or "ring finger"; gitnang daliri (hinlalato accepted when typed)
- kuwaderno or notebook
- nag-iisa or nalulungkot for "lonely"; panatag for "relieved"
- family tree: "kapatid na lalaki / kapatid na babae" for the younger brother and sister (bunso accepted for the sister)
- balakang or baywang for low back pain (balakang = lower back on the back view, hip on the leg view)
- "Ngalay na ngalay na po" or "Nangangalay na po" (shoulder line)
- pulso for wrist (galanggalangan accepted); sikmura for the stomach as an organ; binti for calf

## Signs
- "Kung kailangan" or "Kung kinakailangan" (as-needed label)
- "Para sa panlabas na gamit lamang" or "Pampahid lamang"
- "Pila ng pasahero" or "Pilahan ng pasahero"
- "Para sa mga babae lamang" or "Para sa kababaihan lamang" (train car)
- "Pasensya na, walang barya" or "Walang panukli"
- "Bawal kumaliwa" or the English "No left turn"
- "Tingi at pakyawan": is it really posted?
- "Bawal ang utang" or "Walang utang"
- Kept as written, please confirm: Bawal ang bata sa ward; Bawal sumabit; Bilihan ng tiket; Pakisulat po ang pangalan sa logbook; Bawal hawakan ang paninda

## Machine panels
- "I-warm mo na lang" or "I-keep warm mo na lang" (rice cooker); "I-swing mo" (fan)
- "Pakibawasan po ang lamig ng aircon" = TEMP +
- "Sa pinakababa na po tayo" = G (lift)
- "Tama po ang halagang na-type ninyo" = ENTER (ATM)
- twin tub: "Palabasin mo na ang tubig" (drain), "Ilipat mo na sa spinner para mapiga"

## Body map
- zero / ten or sero / sampu in the 0 to 10 question and caption
- English of makirot (sharp or sore), mahapdi, parang kinukuryente, nangangalay

## Home, machines, food (picture sets)
- bicycle bell: busina, bell or kampanilya; rear rack: rack or angkasan; kickstand: stand
- house wall: dingding or pader
- "altapresyon" or "high blood" in the salt line
- bedside lamp: lampshade or lampara; cable: kable, cord or wire
- toilet paper: tissue or toilet paper; car trunk: compartment or trunk
- the jeepney card uses a jeep emoji (phones have no jeepney picture): keep or drop
- the pan-of-food emoji for ulam; the water drop for tubig
- the chopsticks line "Sa Japan, chopsticks ang gamit nila…": keep or make it local
- Japanese things in the drawings are labelled bathtub, mangkok ng kanin, mangkok ng sabaw, chopsticks, tasa, tsarera

## Town, animals, nature (picture sets)
- street pin 2: intersection, krosing or kanto (kanto is also the corner at pin 5)
- station: "staff ng istasyon" or guard / empleyado; platform, turnstile kept as English
- hospital floor plan: front desk "information", admitting or registration; "consultation room"; kiosk "tindahan" or canteen; "nurse station"
- weather card: temperatura or temperature
- bee sting: "Kinagat" or "Natusok ng bubuyog"
- elephant's trunk as "nguso"

## Picture lessons
- "Na kay Ate ang susi" or "Kay Ate" (the common spoken "Nasa kay Ate" is not taught)
- prices: "Sixty po" or "Sisenta po"
- "Alas-diyes po sila dumating" or "Dumating po sila nang alas-diyes"
- "Mabigat po ang dibdib ko" or "Parang may nakadagan sa dibdib ko"
- "Wala po akong ganang kumain" or "Wala po akong gana"
- maliit or mababa for a short person (the lesson says pandak can sound rude)
- "Lampas sa CR" or "Paglampas ng CR"
- nakasandal or nakatagilid for the leaning book
- sikmura or tiyan for the stomach as an organ
- hyphen rule taught: naka-jacket, naka-face mask (English words) but nakatsinelas, nakasapatos
